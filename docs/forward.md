# Forward paper

A run's locked test is one day of recorded history, and the day was chosen before the run. It cannot say whether the market that paid on that day still pays. Forward paper asks that question with the judge's own engine: a recorder writes the live public market data of the pair on this machine, hour by hour, and `blinded forward` judges the run's final, frozen, on each recorded day. The rows did not exist when the strategy was released, so no choice of day, distance or size can have read them. The fills are still the engine's, so forward paper says whether the market changed, not whether a print would have reached a real order.

```text
blinded record --out DIR [--coin ATUSDT] [--hours H]
blinded forward RUN_DIR --live-data DIR [--latency-ms N] [--watch]
blinded forward RUN_DIR --segment test [--latency-ms N]
blinded forward serve RUN_DIR [--port 8766]
```

`blinded record` needs the `live` extra (`pip install 'blinded[live]'`, which brings `aiohttp`). It touches public endpoints only: MEXC's and Binance's market-data websockets, their `GET /api/v3/time`, and Binance's `GET /api/v3/depth`. It holds no key and sends no order.

## The recorder

One coin on two spot venues, the arbitrage of {doc}`archetypes` (`cross_venue_making`): MEXC is the quote venue, Binance the hedge venue. Per venue one websocket connection carries every stream: MEXC's `aggre.bookTicker@10ms`, `aggre.deals@10ms`, `aggre.depth@10ms` and `limit.depth@20`, each frame a protobuf `PushDataV3ApiWrapper`; Binance's `bookTicker`, `trade` and `depth@100ms` from `data-stream.binance.vision`, JSON.

**The clock.** Every row is stamped with `time.time_ns()` on the raw frame, before it is parsed, and the stamp is never corrected. Let $c$ be this clock's constant offset from true time and $d_v$ the one-way delay from venue $v$: a row's stamp is $t_\text{true} + d_v + c$. One observer stamps both venues, so $c$ moves every row by the same amount and leaves untouched the order of rows across venues, the fill engine, every as-of read, the grid and the hedge's walk. What it does move is `age_ms`, the receive stamp less the exchange's, $d_v + c$, which the theo reads as a quote's staleness. So the recorder measures $c$: eight samples a venue of `GET /api/v3/time` at start and every hour, the offset of each being the server's time less the midpoint of the request's round trip, as NTP takes it. It refuses to start when the median is over 250 ms against either venue, with the line "the recorder's clock is {x} ms off {venue}; fix the clock, the stamps are never corrected", and marks an hour `clock` when its median is. The offset's uncertainty is the round trip's asymmetry, about half of a 300 ms round trip on a home link.

**What is kept.** MEXC pushes its book ticker every 10 ms whether or not the book moved, so a book row is kept only when `version` changes, which is the cadence of Tardis's `book_ticker`. A deals frame of $k$ items gives $k$ trades on the frame's stamp, in frame order, each with its own deal time as the exchange stamp. Binance's `bookTicker` carries no exchange stamp; every one is kept, with `age_ms` 0 as in the Tardis file. Its depth stream is applied to a book seeded from the REST snapshot by Binance's rule: an event whose `u` is at or under the last applied update id is dropped, and every one applied must have `U <= u' + 1 <= u`, where `u'` is the last applied id, the snapshot's `lastUpdateId` for the first. Binance's rule asks a later event to start exactly at `u' + 1`; one that starts earlier and ends later is a second connection's copy of events already applied, and since each level's quantity is absolute, applying it leaves the book where the events it repeats had put it. A depth row of the top 25 levels is kept whenever they change. MEXC's `limit.depth@20` rows feed only the hour's check of the snapshot against the ticker: no sim reads the quote venue's depth.

**Gaps.** A gap row names the venue, the stream, what was seen and the receive stamps either side. It is written for a sequence break (MEXC's depth `fromVersion` against the previous `toVersion`; Binance's trade ids and depth `U`), for a silence of MEXC's 100-a-second ticker over 2 s, which is a dead socket whatever the pair does, and for a lost connection. A Binance depth gap clears the book and asks for a new snapshot; no depth row is written until the book is rebuilt, and the rebuild closes the gap with a `resync` row. Both venues end a connection after 24 hours, so every 12 hours the recorder opens a new one, waits for its first frame and only then drops the old one, never waiting for a close handshake. While two connections are open every stream drops what it has already seen by its own sequence field (the ticker's `version` or `u`, the deal's id, the depth's update ids), so the two give one stream.

**Hour by hour.** Rows are held a column at a time in typed arrays and written at each hour as part files, `DIR/YYYY-MM-DD/parts/HH.N.<stream>.parquet`, with a section of `DATA-raw.md` (rows per stream, frames received, gap rows, the clock samples, and `blinded demo arb`'s checks over the hour: each venue's spread, touch and prints, the two venues against each other, each depth stream against its touch, who prints) and a line of `hours.jsonl`. At 00:05 UTC the previous day's parts become the three files `blinded demo arb` writes, built in a separate process by the same function, {func}`blinded.demo_mm.arb_frames`: the data file, the quote venue's touch file and the hedge venue's depth file, with a brief and the day's section. The parts are kept: they hold the quote venue's own depth, which no built file does. The Tardis AT day fed row by row through this writer gives the three parquet files of `blinded demo arb` byte for byte, which is the test of the layout.

## Judging a day

`blinded forward` refuses a run whose test has not been scored. From the vault it takes the brief, the judging rules, the final's code and its features' code (the vault's copies, each checked against its hash), and the theo's fitted parameters when the brief declares a theo. For each recorded day it writes `vault/forward/YYYY-MM-DD.brief.yaml`, the vault's brief with only these changed: the day's three files, the grid's start at the day's 00:00, the latencies, one window `forward` from 00:05 to the next 00:00 (the brief's 5-minute warm-up), and a `forward:` block naming the run, the final and the hashes of what was read. The theo, when declared, is filtered over the day with train's parameters, never refitted.

The latencies are the day's measured ones: the p90 round trip to MEXC for the order, cancel and response latencies, the p90 to Binance for the hedge's, each rounded up to the 100 ms grid step. They are the slow end of what was measured, since an order goes by REST. `--latency-ms` sets all four instead.

The panel is built from two hours of the day before (on the first recorded day there is none, and its first two hours are not scored) to the last closed hour plus the five minutes the hedges and markouts read. The quotes are the final's code evaluated as the daemon evaluates it, in a fresh worker of {mod}`blinded.isolate`'s pool, behind the audit hook and inside `srt`; without `srt` forward paper refuses exactly as the daemon does, unless `BLINDED_UNFENCED=1`, and nothing is written. The pass is the judge's: {func}`blinded.sim_maker.arb_models` for the two queue models, the worse scored, the vault's judging rules. It is one continuous pass from 00:05, re-run from the start at each hour, so the stock's withdrawals and rebalances see the day's drift as one account would and a day's numbers are one pass, not 24 restarted ones. The hourly series are the pass's per-step net summed by the hour of its decision and its fills counted by the hour of the fill.

**The known answer.** `blinded forward RUN_DIR --segment test` judges the vault's own test segment the same way, with validation's last two hours as its warm-up. At the vault's latencies it must give the locked test's numbers: on the published AT pair at real transfer fees, 161 fills, +18.8 bps a fill, net 2.4411550499999444 USD, and 4 transfers costing 0.3221 USD, every key of the report's `final.arb` and `final.transfers` equal.

## Hours that are not judged

An hour is `unobserved` when no part covers all of it (the recorder was not running, or started inside it), `gapped` when a gap row's span touches it, `clock` when its clock was over 250 ms, and `warmup` for the first recorded day's first two hours. Such an hour is printed hatched with its reason; its net is not added, its fills are not counted, and the CUSUM does not move on it. The pass still runs through it, since the engine needs contiguous rows. Leaving an hour out hurts a strategy whose excluded hours were good and helps one whose excluded hours were bad; which hours are excluded depends on the recording, never on the P&L. A day with more than four excluded hours gets no day row, only "too few hours".

## The verdict

The reference is the locked test recomputed by this engine at the forward latencies, its whole hours' net and fills, so both sides use one engine and one latency. For a judged hour $t$ with net $x_t$, $z_t = (x_t - m)/s$ with $m$ and $s$ the reference hours' mean and sd, and the lower CUSUM is

$$C_t = \max(0,\; C_{t-1} - z_t - k), \qquad C_0 = 0,$$

with an alarm at the first $C_t > h$; the same runs on hourly fills, $k = 0.5$. Were $m$ and $s$ known, $h = 5$ would do: under no change and Gaussian $z$, Siegmund's approximation gives an average run length of $(e^{2kh} - 2kh - 1)/(2k^2) = 285$ hours, and {func}`blinded.forward.false_alarms`, bootstrapping weeks of 168 hours from the AT day's 22 whole hours (a third of their 5-minute blocks are zero, so not Gaussian), alarms in 9.2% of weeks on the net and 9.9% on fills (an independent bootstrap gave 9.5% and 9.9%).

They are not known. They are the mean and sd of the locked test's whole hours, five for the AT pair, and a mean from five hours is off by 0.45 sd on average, which the CUSUM reads as drift. Drawing each simulated week's reference from the same hours as the week (`n=` in {func}`blinded.forward.false_alarms`), $h = 5$ alarms in 43% of unchanged weeks on the AT day's net with a 5-hour reference and 28% with a 22-hour one. So each threshold is set by {func}`blinded.forward.threshold`: the 90th percentile of a week's largest $C_t$ with the reference's error counted, which holds an unchanged strategy to 10% of weeks. On those 22 hours it is 38.8 for a 5-hour reference, 7.9 for a 22-hour one, and 5.0 with $m$ and $s$ known.

{func}`blinded.forward.reference` bootstraps the thresholds from the whole vault day's hours, recomputed at the forward latencies, when that day has more whole hours than the test. A CUSUM standardised by its own sample's mean and sd alarms equally often whatever the hours' level and spread, so the hours of train and validation, which the search saw, lend only the shape of an hour's net and fills (their skew and their zeros), never the level the forward hours are held to. What the thresholds cost is then computed against the page's own reference: the same shape moved to the locked test's mean and sd, then halved or stopped. A stop adds $m/s - k$ to $C_t$ every hour, so it is caught at the first hour past $h/(m/s - k)$.

For the AT pair at 400 ms and 800 ms on 21 Oct, the locked test's five hours give a mean net of 0.372 USD an hour with an sd of 0.349. The thresholds are 43.5 on the net and 46.2 on fills, where $h = 5$ would have alarmed in 48% and 52% of unchanged weeks. A stop is caught in 77 hours on the net and 64 on fills. A halving moves the hourly net by half its mean, 0.53 of its sd, less than the reference's own error, and is not caught within a week. No sequential rule fixes that, because the limit is the locked test's 5.9 hours. A week of forward hours measures the strategy's own hourly net about five times as tightly as the test did (the square root of 168 over 5.9), which is what the forward hours' own interval below uses.

The page says "the forward period disagrees with the locked test" when either CUSUM is over its $h$, naming the statistic, the hour it crossed and the two means; otherwise "agrees so far" with the judged hours, the net against the expected and the fills against the expected. It prints both thresholds, the rate $h = 5$ would have had, what a stop and a halving would take, and the locked test's own 90% interval scaled to the judged hours, with the forward total beside it.

The CUSUM asks whether the forward hours differ from the locked test's. The page also asks whether they make money: the forward hours' own 90% interval for the mean hourly net, a studentised bootstrap with each judged hour a block, says whether it lies above zero, across it or below it. It waits for 20 judged hours, the count the judge's fee verdict asks of its blocks. An hour can be its own block because an hour's net barely depends on the last: on the AT day's hedged 5-minute block nets, the variance of an hour's sum is 2.88 times that of twelve independent blocks and of two hours' sum 2.92 times that of twenty-four, so the dependence is spent within the hour. Drawn 20, 48 and 168 hours at a time from that day's hours, the interval covered the mean 90 to 92% of the time. A halved strategy still lies above zero within a week, and the interval shows how much it makes.

## Where it is written

Everything goes under `vault/forward/`, which the hook denies by path and the OS sandbox by directory ({doc}`sandbox`), and of which `DATA.md`, `LEDGER.md` and `NOTES.md` say nothing: the forward briefs, each day's theo, `fence.jsonl` (each judgement's start and whether `srt` fenced it), `ledger.sqlite` and `forward.html`. The ledger has a row for every hour of every judgement, a day row per judgement and the reference; rows are only added, and the page shows the latest of each with the number of judgements a day had. `blinded forward serve` serves the page on 127.0.0.1 and nothing else. The page is static, no JavaScript, its charts inline SVG through {mod}`blinded.plots`: the hourly net and fills with excluded hours hatched, the cumulative forward net against the locked test's mean and interval, both CUSUMs with their thresholds, where the last day's P&L went (capture, hedge delay, slippage, fees), and the stock on both venues. Forward paper is started by a person, never by the driver.
