# The report

`blinded report DIR` writes `DIR/report.html`: one self-contained page with inline CSS and inline SVG charts, no JavaScript and no network, so it opens offline and survives email. It never embeds a data row, only aggregates of rows. Beside it goes `report.json`, the numbers the page shows, which `blinded replay DIR` recomputes from the data to check.

## The sections, in order

1. **Verdict.** One sentence on the locked test: the final strategy, its net bps/hour with the fee verdict's 90% interval (below), its Sharpe per square-root hour, and whether it is viable as a taker. Then the test table, with the brief's row and the three unrealistic diagnostic rows (latency 0, fees 0, both) and the maker-proxy upper bound, each marked, the `maker-taker` row where the file has a touch stream ({doc}`sim`), and the cumulative test P&L. For a market-making final the sentence names the queue model the judge's rule took, the Sharpe that rule scores (with its block when the strategy's holds outlast the brief's), the gross before the maker fee, and, when it lost, why: the maker fee and the mid's move against the fills within 10 s, each as a multiple of the window's median half spread; the test table has the four models, latency 0 and the fee rows. If a probe halted the run, or nothing was released, the verdict says so and there is no test number. In a run with families the verdict names the final strategy's family and legs, and for a pair its P&L is on one unit of notional per leg, with its gross exposure beside it. In a run with archetypes it names the archetype and its edge too, and on a bar file the line under it says the fills are a model ({doc}`sim`).
2. **What was tried.** Distinct features and strategies, accepted and rejected, over how many iterations, how long and how much (from the sessions' own `total_cost_usd`). The Ladder chart: every feature's validation rank IC in order, which the agent never saw, and the released best as a step, which is all it was told. Train against validation rank IC for every accepted feature, with the line y = x. Each strategy's validation Sharpe in order with the best so far, which is what a loop without a judge would ship; in a naive run, the Sharpe the loop saw. A run with families has a table first: for each family, its features and strategies submitted, accepted and released, its released best of each kind, its sessions in each phase, and which family holds the final strategy. A run with {doc}`archetypes` has the menu's line ("4 of 10 archetypes run on this data, in 2 of 5 edges") and the same table by edge, with each edge's archetypes, then a table per edge with a row per runnable archetype: its features and strategies submitted, accepted and released, its best validation score of each kind, which the agent never saw, and whether it holds the final strategy. Below them, folded, "What this data cannot test": the refusal line of every archetype the file cannot run. `report.json` keeps all of it under `archetypes`: `menu`, `cost_model`, `edges` and `trials`.
3. **Deflation.** The chosen strategy's validation Sharpe, the expected maximum under the null for the trial count, the deflated-Sharpe probability, and PBO or the reason there are too few trials or blocks ({func}`blinded.deflate.summary`). A protected run with archetypes prints first $N$, the distinct strategies over the whole run with its error rows and how many of those were refused archetypes, $K$, the edges, the strategies each edge tried and what each released: each edge has its own Ladder, so the run leaks up to $K$ times the bits of one.
4. **The signal.** One directional feature and one directional strategy, each on its own instrument, shown on train and validation, and on test only when the final strategy was scored there. The feature is the final strategy's, else the last one the Ladder released, else the accepted one with the largest train rank IC; the strategy is the final one, else the accepted one with the best validation Sharpe. For the feature: the markout fan, the decay and the IC per 30-minute window. For the strategy: its P&L three ways and its fill markout. A run with a lead-lag family then has a cross-correlation chart and table for every leader and follower, and the family's own feature and strategy chosen by the same rule; a run with a pairs family has four charts for a pair ({doc}`families`). Then the maker-proxy upper bound. A run with archetypes is laid out by edge and then by archetype instead, each archetype with its own pick by the same rule kept to its trials, the charts of its mechanism (one leg's feature and strategy; for lead-lag the curve of its own leader and follower; for a pairs archetype the four pairs charts, drawn at the template's parameters on the implied pair with the shortest median half-life on train when no strategy was accepted, and for cross-venue arbitrage the cross-correlation across its two venues; for a basket archetype its feature's cross-sectional rank IC by horizon and by window with the fan of the basket it implies, and its strategy's net P&L leg by leg with the fan of its rebalances, drawn at the template's parameters on a default score when no strategy was accepted; for the basis trade the four pairs charts on its declared pair, shaded by its strategy or by `bl.carry` at its parameters), and its "How it dies" panel: the page's sentence on how the archetype dies, then each of its death checks with a column per segment ({doc}`archetypes`). Market making has its own panel. Its strategy's bracket: a table per segment of the four fill models' fills, units, captured half spread, markout at 10 s, inventory beyond it, fees, net per hour and Sharpe on the brief's blocks, then for RA and PR the Sharpe the judge's rule scores and the blocks it is measured on, the scored model marked and its Sharpe in bold, and a line per segment saying which model the rule took and why, whether the brief's blocks would have picked the other, and on validation whether the strategy's holds left fewer than twenty of its blocks so that the Ladder was given no score. Every segment is run on the panel the judge scored it on, so the page's validation numbers are the ledger's and its test numbers the verdict's. Then the fee rows on the scored model's fills, and three charts: the half hour of the last segment with the most fills (the touch as two steps, the resting quotes as lines, fills as markers, the inventory below); the markout of each fill model's resting fills from 10 s before to 60 s after, RA and PR solid, T and F dashed, with the scored model's fill against the mid at 0 and its break-even, the maker fee less what it captured; and where its P&L goes, the cumulative capture, that plus the mid's move in the 10 s after each fill, and the net. Then "How it dies" with D1, D3, D4, D6, D7, D12 and D13.
5. **Failed ideas and why.** The rejected trials grouped by reason, with each idea's own one-line hypothesis, so the user can see what the data does not support.
6. **Naive comparison.** Present only when a naive run sits beside the protected one (a `NAME-naive` directory, or a `naive/` sibling): the naive loop's claimed numbers against the protected judge's score of its final pick on the same locked test, and the two runs' judge-call counts, in total and, with families, for each family. Below the table it names the naive pick, the window its claim was made on, and whether the agent chose it or the driver took the best claimed Sharpe because the agent named none.
7. **Reproduce.** The split boundaries and each segment's SHA-256, the brief, and the final feature and strategy code, with the one-line `blinded replay` command. The data file is never embedded.
8. **Appendix.** The probe count (expected 0), the full ledger as a table, and NOTES.md and DATA.md folded away.

## Replay

Everything the page derives from the data, the test verdict, the cumulative P&L path and every number behind the signal charts, is computed once by {func}`blinded.report.recompute` from the vault: the final strategy and its feature are read from their vault copies and scored on the test segment with the brief's latency and fees, and the diagnostics come from {mod}`blinded.markouts`. Those numbers go into `report.json`.

`blinded replay DIR` runs `recompute` again and compares every number against `report.json`, every point of every chart included (a few thousand numbers on a 14-hour day; the family charts sit under `recompute.diagnostics.lead_lag` and `recompute.diagnostics.pairs`, and an archetypes run's charts and death checks under `recompute.diagnostics.archetypes[edge][archetype]`, a market maker's bracket, fans, decompositions, P&L lines and excerpt under its `maker`), and the naive run's locked-test rows when the page shows a naive comparison. In a run with archetypes it also recomputes the menu from the vault's brief and training rows and checks every verdict, refusal line and number of it against the menu the agent was shown. Because the computation is deterministic, a faithful report matches to within a tight tolerance; a mismatch names the number and fails. When nothing was scored on test there is nothing data-derived to rebuild, and replay says that instead of reporting a match. A test builds a run, writes the report and asserts replay agrees on every number. No number on the page is taken on trust: each one can be rebuilt from the user's own copy of the data.

## The charts

Drawn with matplotlib ({mod}`blinded.plots`) and written into the page as SVG text, so the page stays one file. Each chart's numbers come from `report.json`, so replay checks what is drawn.

- **Markout fan.** Each time the feature went past its 5th or 95th percentile on train, taken as a trade in the direction it predicts (the sign of its train rank IC), with events at least 70 s apart ({func}`blinded.markouts.events`). The line is the average mid move in the trade's direction, from 10 s before the decision to 60 s after, on every grid step at 100 ms or coarser ({func}`blinded.markouts.mid_moves`, {func}`blinded.markouts.fan`). The band is two standard errors either side, so it widens as single paths drift apart, and because the gap is as long as the window no two paths share a second of price, so the standard error is a fair one. Longs and shorts are drawn apart. Left of zero is how the price got there. The dotted line is the fill. The dashed line is the move at the fill plus the spread crossed twice, which is where a taker who fills there and crosses back out breaks even before fees; the dash-dot line adds the fees, and a line more than three times the fan's height is named in the legend instead of drawn. A table under the chart gives the mean and two standard errors at 5 s and 1 s before, at the fill, and at 1, 5, 10, 30 and 60 s.
- **Decay.** Rank IC against horizon, one line per segment.
- **IC per window.** Rank IC at the primary horizon in each 30-minute clock window, coloured by segment.
- **Where the P&L goes.** The strategy's cumulative P&L from the mid move alone, after the spread, and after the spread and fees, one panel per segment. The gap between two lines is what that cost took.
- **Order markout fan.** Every order the strategy sent, on the clock of the send: the mid move in the order's direction, weighted by size, from 10 s before to 60 s after, with two standard errors either side and buys and sells apart. Orders less than 70 s apart share prices, so the standard error treats each such run as one draw ({func}`blinded.markouts.clusters`). A mean rising into zero means the strategy buys after the price has gone up. The diamond is the average fill, the half spread plus whatever moved in flight ({func}`blinded.markouts.entry_cost`); the dashed line is that fill plus the fee, which the line has to clear for an order to pay. The same table sits under it.
- **Lead-lag.** One chart per leader and follower: the correlation of the leader's last step with the follower's step ending a lag later, against the lag, one line per segment ({func}`blinded.diagnostics.lead_lag_curve`). Left of zero the follower moved first. The shaded strip is the time to a taker's fill, latency plus feed delay; the dotted line is the follower's first whole step after it, the step a taker holds. Below it, per segment, the lag at the peak, the correlation there and on that step, the best from there on, and whether a taker can use it.
- **Pairs.** The spread less its walk-forward mean against the bands it traded on, with the position held under it; the hedge ratio per clock window; the half-life per clock window, open circles where the spread did not revert, against what a random walk reads in a window as long; and the markout fan of the spread: each time its z-score went past the strategy's entry band, a trade back towards the mean at the hedge ratio of the moment, against the spread of both legs crossed twice and their fees ({doc}`families`).
- **Funding.** For a basis trade, D7's two cumulative lines on each segment: the funding the perpetual leg received, positive, or paid, and the mid P&L of both legs, the basis change ({func}`blinded.plots.funding`). A basis trade is paid by the first and dies when the second falls further than the first rises.
- **Baskets.** For a basket feature, its cross-sectional rank IC (the rank correlation across the legs of the feature with each leg's forward return, averaged over the window) against horizon and in each window of the block, and the markout fan of `bl.basket` on it at the archetype's parameters. For a basket strategy, the cumulative net P&L of each leg as a thin line and the basket's as a bold one, one panel per segment ({func}`blinded.plots.basket_pnl`), and the fan of its rebalances: at each step where a leg's position changes, the move of every leg weighted by its new position over the gross, so one notional across the legs, with rebalances closer than the fan's gap counted as one draw.
- **The Ladder**, **train against validation**, **strategies in order** and **the locked test**, as in sections 1 and 2.

The fill on every chart drawn on the clock of the send is the latency plus the instrument's feed delay after the decision; the order markout's diamond sits on it. On a bar file, where the fill is a whole bar after the decision, the fans run a bar for each of their seconds: on hourly bars from 10 hours before the decision to 60 hours after, events 70 hours apart, the axis in hours, and the IC per window is per window of the brief's block. On a file thinned by `blinded demo binance` the decay and markout charts leave out the horizons and offsets whose reads the file does not keep, such as 100 ms and 250 ms on its 500 ms grid, so every number drawn is exact ({doc}`demo-data`).

## An arbitrage

An archetype run's section 4 draws `cross_venue_making`'s pick on each segment, on the panel the judge scored it on: the four fill models in the probe's terms (fills, USD traded, and per fill the captured, delay, slippage, fees and net in bps of the fill at the hedge venue's mid, the picked-off share, the net USD a day, the buys and the drift), with the hold rule's Sharpe and blocks and the scored model marked; the sim rows (`RA uncapped`, `latency 0`, `hedge 1 s` and the hedge fee rows); the scored model's first 20 fills on the last segment, each decomposed; the half hour of the last segment with the most fills, both venues' touches with the resting quotes, each fill and the hedge it sent; and the cumulative capture, after the delay, after the slippage and net, in USD. Its "How it dies" panel is D1, D3, D4, D6 and D14 to D17 ({doc}`archetypes`). An arbitrage final's verdict names the pair, the model scored, the net a fill after the hedge fee and in USD at the quote's size with the interval, the picked-off share, the drift and the counterparty's prints a day; every number is in `report.recompute` and `blinded replay` recomputes it.

## The fee verdict

Every final verdict, and D1 of every strategy with a sim table on each segment, says which of three the strategy is. Fees do not change the fills of a fixed policy, so per block $b$ the net at a fee $f$ is $\text{gross}_b - f\,u_b + c_b$, with $u_b$ the units the fee is charged on (a maker's resting units at its maker fee, a position strategy's units traded at its taker fee) and $c_b$ what does not move with $f$ (funding, borrow, a maker's flatten at the taker fee). The blocks are the score's: the scored model's (for a maker, the worse of RA and PR by the hold rule; for a position strategy, the brief's sim row) under the hold rule and the run's judging rules (under rules 2 the remainder folded into the last, under rules 1 dropped; see the judging rules below), and the draws are the score's, {class}`blinded.ladder.BlockDraw` with the brief's `n_boot` and `seed`. Each replicate's net per hour is linear and decreasing in $f$, so the 90% interval's lower end is too, and $f^*$, the fee at which it is zero, is found by bisection.

An interval claims something only on as many blocks as the Ladder needs to release a strategy, {data}`blinded.archetypes.PERIODS` (20) whole blocks of the score's block. With fewer the page says *too few blocks to say*, how many blocks there are, and the break-even fee $f_0$ alone; a pass that never filled has no verdict.

- *Profitable at the brief's fee*: the lower end is above zero at the brief's fee.
- *Fee-limited*: it is not, but $f^*$ is at or above some tier of the fee table. The page says "consistently profitable at a maker (or taker) fee of $f^*$ bps or lower", gives the break-even fee $f_0$, where the mean net is zero, and names every tier at or under $f^*$, the strategy's venue and products first, then the others. The venue and product come from the brief's `fee_tier`; without one the venue comes from the brief's name and each product from an instrument the pass trades, a `_PERP` name a USD-M perpetual and any other spot ({func}`blinded.fees.product_of`), so a spot and perpetual carry lists both of its venue's products as its own.
- *Edge-limited*: $f^*$ is below every published tier, a deeper rebate than any venue in the table offers, or no fee brings the lower end to zero. The page says which case: it *loses before fees* ($f_0 < 0$), or it is *positive before fees but not significant at any fee a venue offers* ($f_0 \ge 0$, and the lower end is below zero even at the deepest tier on its side).

For an arbitrage the fee that matters is the hedge's: the verdict is on the hedge fee, side taker, charged on the units hedged in each step, with the quote venue's maker fee held at the brief's and the rebalance fees kept in $c_b$, and the venue and product whose tiers it names are the hedge instrument's (`ATUSDT_BINANCE` is Binance spot; {func}`blinded.fees.judge_arb`).

The page prints one caveat beside it: the fills are the strategy as written, and a strategy built for a rebate would quote differently. The verdict line prints this interval too, the page's only one, and says *too few blocks to say for an interval* where the fee verdict does. A page written before 20 Oct 2026 has a different statistic on that line, its own interval from 40 equal pieces of the test path drawn independently with a seed of their own. The interval is studentised (bootstrap-t): each draw's total is centred on the pass's and divided by its own standard error, and the quantiles of that ratio scale the pass's error. On the statistics audit's design (`mc_sharpe.py`: unit-variance step P&L, iid or AR(1) at 0.9, 60-step blocks) it covers 87 to 90% at a nominal 90% on 10 to 41 blocks, where a percentile interval covers 83 to 88%, and 87 to 93% on 2 to 5 blocks, against 58 to 80%. The verdict is in `report.json` (`recompute.final.fee_verdict`, and `fee_verdict` under each segment of D1) and `blinded replay` recomputes its numbers. It is never in a daemon response, the menu, RULES.md, LEDGER.md or any file in the agent's workspace, protected or naive (`tests/test_maker_run.py`).


## The judging rules

A run is judged under one set of rules, named in its manifest as `judge_rules` ({data}`blinded.ladder.RULES`). `blinded init` writes the current rules, 3, into `vault/manifest.json`, which the ledger records at init and the daemon checks when it starts, and into the workspace's copy of the brief, so `blinded eval` there judges as the daemon does. A run whose manifest names none was judged under rules 1. {meth}`blinded.vault.RunDir.brief` gives every judging path the manifest's version: the daemon, `blinded report`, `blinded replay`, the menu's check and a protected run's naive twin, which is read under its own manifest. The agent cannot change it: the vault is denied to it, and an edit to the workspace's brief changes only its own `blinded eval`.

- *Rules 1*, every run before those below: a block Sharpe takes the whole blocks from the window's start and drops the remainder, in the point estimate and the replicates, and only a trade fills a resting quote.
- *Rules 2* (20 Oct 2026): the remainder folds into the last block ({func}`blinded.ladder.fold`), and a book row whose far touch reaches a resting quote's price fills its remainder there (under T only strictly through it), so D12 has a crossed share.
- *Rules 3* (20 Oct 2026), after three audits: in the fill engine a trade comes before the book row on its stamp, the row that already shows it (read first, the row clamped the queue ahead to the post-trade size and the trade then took from it again: on the simulator audit's hand example 1 unit filled where none should); a taker pass pays to leave the position it holds at the window's end, crossed at the far touch at the end plus the latency with the taker fee, and a maker-taker pass flattens what is left; every fee is charged on its trade's notional over $P_\text{ref}$, as spread, funding and borrow are; the Ladder's first release needs the score above twice its own bootstrap standard deviation as well as the step, and a later release must beat the best's unrounded value as well as its rounded one; a hedge the depth cannot price is priced past B's far touch by a spread, never at the mid, and the part of a hedge past the depth's last level a further spread beyond it; an arbitrage's USDT transfer pays `withdrawal_fee_usdt`, and the judge refuses an arbitrage until both withdrawal fees are set.

The page says under the verdict which rules judged the run: "Judged under rules v2", or for a run judged under rules 1 "Judged under rules v1", and when it has a final, what the final's locked test shows under rules 2: its net and the Sharpe its verdict line prints (a market maker's under the worse of RA and PR by the hold rule, named), or that it does not trade there. Those numbers are in `report.json` under `recompute.rules_v2`, the run's version under `judge_rules`, and `blinded replay` recomputes them. A run judged under rules older than the current ones also gets a line of its own, `verdict.rules_current`: "Under the current rules (v3: ...) this final's locked-test net is X bps/hour (Sharpe Y)", from `recompute.rules_v3`, or that the current rules do not score it (an arbitrage whose brief sets no USDT withdrawal fee). Neither key changes anything else: the run stays judged, and every other number on the page stays measured, under its own rules. A market maker's verdict line reads its net, gross, model, hold-rule Sharpe and interval from one source, the recompute under the run's rules.
